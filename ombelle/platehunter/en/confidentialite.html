Your restaurant memories stay private

PlateHunter Privacy Policy · Last updated: November 8, 2026

PlateHunter stores what you enter to show your restaurant notebook, podiums and stats, and what you share with the people at your meals. This information is never sold and never used for advertising. The free version shows Google AdMob ads, which receive device data (advertising ID, IP address), never your restaurants or ratings.

Who is responsible

PlateHunter is published by Maxence Bonnet, sole proprietor (MaxFabrics, SIREN 104406707). For any question about your data: ombelle@maxfabrics.com.

Why, and on what legal basis

Your account, your notebook, shared meals and backups are used to run the service you asked for (performance of the contract). Personalised ads, menu reading by an assistant, your location and crash reports rely on your consent, which you can withdraw at any time. Service security and abuse prevention rely on our legitimate interest. Purchases follow our legal obligations.

What you save

The restaurants (name, address, location on the map if you place it), visits, dishes, ratings, comments, expenses, photos of dishes and memories, and your display preferences. None of this goes to the ad network, and the app does no audience measurement.

Where your data lives

It is stored in your secure online space, at Google Firebase (servers in Paris), so you can find it again when you change phones. Only you can read your notebook. A meal is shared with the people at the table: they see the place, the menu and the photos, and each person’s ratings once they have rated. Dish and memory photos go through the PlateHunter relay (Cloudflare), which checks that you are at this table, and are kept by storage hosts in Europe: Cloudflare R2, Tigris (Frankfurt), Oracle Cloud (Paris) or Backblaze B2 (Amsterdam). To block fraudulent access, Google (App Check and Play Integrity) checks that requests really come from the PlateHunter app.

Menu reading by an assistant

Only if you agree (asked at the first reading, setting in your profile): when you photograph a restaurant’s menu, the photos, without their location, are sent through the PlateHunter relay to an artificial intelligence assistant that reads the dishes and prices: open models hosted by Cloudflare (Workers AI), or OpenRouter’s as a fallback. Cloudflare doesn’t keep these photos after the response and doesn’t use them to train its models; OpenRouter is set to exclude providers that would. The relay limits the number of readings per person. Otherwise, without a connection, or if the assistant doesn’t respond, the menu is read on your phone.

Where to eat?

To suggest restaurants around you, a rounded location (to within about 500 m) is sent to the PlateHunter relay, which passes it on, rounded to the kilometer, to Geoapify (OpenStreetMap data); as a fallback, your phone directly queries OpenStreetMap’s Overpass service. The restaurants found, their distances and a summary of your table’s tastes (cuisines, ratings, budget, favorites), with no person’s name, are then sent to the assistant, the same way as menu reading, to rank them, if the online assistant is on; otherwise, or beyond the daily limit, sorting happens on your phone. A Hunter’s tastes are deduced only from the ratings they revealed to you at your shared meals.

Map and addresses

Map backgrounds come from OpenFreeMap (OpenStreetMap data): your phone downloads the tiles for the displayed area from their servers, or as a fallback from OpenStreetMap’s, which therefore see your IP address and that area. Searching for an address, and looking up the address of a point on the map, go through your phone’s built-in location service (provided by Google on most Android phones).

Your location

If you allow it, it is read when you tap “My location”, when you open the map or “Where to eat?”, to center the map or sort restaurants by distance. It is never tracked in the background. The location of a restaurant you place is saved in your notebook.

Advertising

PlateHunter is free thanks to advertising: now and then, an ad in your notebook, shaped like a restaurant card and labelled “Ad”, never while you’re entering something; and, only if you choose it, a video that gives you a little more when you reach a limit. The ads come from Google AdMob. Before any ad, the app asks for your choice through Google’s consent form; with your consent, Google receives technical information about your phone (advertising ID, IP address, model, language, interactions with the ads) to show the ads, limit how often they repeat, measure them and, if you accept it, personalize them. If you refuse, ads still appear, but non-personalized, or “limited” if you also refuse storage on your phone (without an advertising ID or tracker); Google processes this information under its own rules (policies.google.com/technologies/ads). Your restaurants, ratings, photos and comments are never sent to it. In the European Union, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, you can change your mind at any time: your profile › Ad choices. You can reset or delete the advertising ID in Android settings. With “Remove ads”, no ads appear: only the video is still offered, if you choose it. PlateHunter Plus shows no ads.

Purchases

Purchases, such as “Remove ads” and the “plus” subscription (monthly or yearly), go through Google Play, which handles the payment: PlateHunter never sees your card or payment method. To grant you the purchase, the app sends PlateHunter’s server the purchase token provided by Google Play; the server checks it with Google, then keeps a fingerprint (SHA-256) of that token; for a subscription, it also keeps the token itself, needed to check each renewal with Google. It also keeps the product bought and your account’s identifier, without your name or email address, for as long as the purchase is valid. A refunded purchase or an expired subscription stops being granted; the subscription can be cancelled at any time in Google Play, and the free limits come back when it ends, without deleting anything. To apply these limits, the server counts, per person, assistant readings, rankings and uploaded photos, without their content. You can get your purchases back with “Restore purchases” in your profile.

Your account

Your account is created with an email address and a password, or with Google. Your name and username are visible to your Hunters and to anyone searching for your exact username; your email address is never shown to them. Your date of birth is used to check that you are 18: we keep only the result, never the date.

What is required

To create an account you need an email address (or a Google account), a name, a username and to confirm that you are 18: without them, there is no account. Everything else (photos, location, ratings, assistant) is optional. No decision with legal effects is made automatically: the “Where to eat?” ranking is only a suggestion.

Who receives your data

Only the providers that run the service, each for the role described here: Google (Firebase, Crashlytics, AdMob, Google Play), Cloudflare (relay, photos, assistant), OpenRouter (assistant, as a fallback), Geoapify (restaurants around you) and the photo hosts listed above. Some of them, including Google, Cloudflare and OpenRouter, may process data outside the European Union, in particular in the United States: these transfers rely on the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses or, for certified companies, on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework. We never sell your data.

Crash reports

Only if you accept (question asked when you arrive, switch in your profile): if the app crashes, a technical report (phone model, Android and app versions, error trace, installation ID) goes to Google Firebase Crashlytics so that we can fix the fault. It contains none of your restaurants, ratings or photos. Without your consent, nothing is sent. These reports are kept for 90 days.

Sharing and backups

What you choose to send (invitation, recap, podium or backup file) goes through the app you choose. A backup contains your notebook, meals, ratings and tie-breaks, without photos; keep it safe. A copy is also kept each week in the app’s private folder on this phone. The app’s data isn’t copied to Android’s automatic backup.

How long

Your data stays as long as your account exists. An item in the trash stays there for 30 days, then it is erased for good the next time the app opens. When you delete your account, what concerns you is erased at once from our servers and from this phone. Our servers’ nightly backup copies are cleared of it within 120 days at most.

Correct or erase your data

You can edit or delete each item from the app; a deleted entry first goes to the trash. To delete your account: your profile › Delete my account. Your notebook, ratings, comments and username are erased; shared restaurants and photos stay with your Hunters, under “Former member”. Without the app: maxfabrics.com/ombelle/platehunter/en/supprimer-mon-compte, or an email to ombelle@maxfabrics.com. You also have the right to access your data, have it corrected, restrict its use, object to it, withdraw your consent and get it back in a readable format (portability): the app’s backup file is one; for anything else, write to ombelle@maxfabrics.com. You can also contact the CNIL (cnil.fr).
