Your library stays yours

BookHunter privacy policy · Last updated: 12 November 2026

BookHunter keeps your library on your phone. Only what is needed to give it back to you on another device (an encrypted copy) and what you share with your friends goes online. What you record in it (books, notes, quotes, photos) is never sold and never used for advertising. The free version shows Google AdMob ads, which receive device data (advertising ID, IP address), never your books, your notes or your quotes.

Who is responsible

BookHunter is published by Maxence Bonnet, sole trader (MaxFabrics, SIREN 104406707), under the Ombelle brand. For any question about your data: ombelle@maxfabrics.com.

Why, and on what basis

Your account, your library, its online copy, your photos, your friends, your loans, your exchanges and your shared reads are used to run the service you asked for (performance of the contract). Purchases also rely on the contract, and keeping their records on our accounting obligations. Personalised ads and reading a photo with an assistant rely on your consent, which you can withdraw at any time; non-personalised ads, on our legitimate interest in funding the free version. Service security, moderation and the fight against abuse rely on our legitimate interest in protecting the service and its members, and on our legal obligations. Your voice recordings never leave your phone: we do not receive them.

What you record

Your books and their records, your reading statuses, your sessions, your notes and reviews, your quotes, your vocabulary list, your goals, your series, your loans and borrowings, your sales, your stories and writing sessions, your photos (covers, personal photos, condition of a lent book) and your voice recordings. All of this is saved on your phone first. None of this goes to the ad network, and the app does no audience measurement.

Where your data lives

Your library lives on your phone. So you can get it back on another device, a copy is sent to the BookHunter relay (Cloudflare), which encrypts it with a key unique to your account before storing it in Cloudflare R2, in the European Union; your photos are stored the same way, encrypted. Your voice recordings stay on your phone and are not part of this copy. What you share with your friends (your profile, your shelf of lendable books, your loans, exchanges, gifts and shared reads) is stored with Google Firebase, in Paris. To block fraudulent access, Google (App Check and Play Integrity) checks that requests really come from the BookHunter app.

What your friends see

Your profile (username, name, photo) is visible to anyone who searches for your exact username, unless you have blocked them; your email address is never shown. Your shelf of lendable books (title, authors, ISBN, cover, number of pages) is visible only to your friends, and your wish list only if you choose to show it to them; never your notes, your review or the price. In a shared read, participants see each other's progress, and published notes only once they have reached that page.

Book search and covers

To find a book by its title, author or ISBN, the search goes through the BookHunter relay, which queries the Bibliothèque nationale de France (French National Library) and Open Library and keeps the answers in cache for 30 days; as a fallback, your phone queries them directly. Covers provided by Open Library are downloaded by your phone from their servers, which therefore see your IP address.

Reading a photo with an assistant

Only if you agree (you are asked before the first time): when you photograph a cover to fill in a record, or handwritten pages to count your words, the photos are sent by the BookHunter relay to an artificial intelligence assistant: open models hosted by Cloudflare (Workers AI) or, as a fallback, those of OpenRouter, set to exclude providers that keep data or use it to train their models. Neither the app nor the relay keeps these photos, and their content is never logged. The relay limits the number of readings per day. Reading a summary or a quote from a photo happens on your phone. A photo is only sent when you start a reading yourself: to withdraw your consent, simply stop starting them.

Advertising

BookHunter is free thanks to advertising: from time to time, an ad in your book lists, marked “Ad”, never while you are typing nor in a loan or an exchange; and, only if you choose it, a video that gives you a little more when you reach a limit. Ads come from Google AdMob. Before any ad, the app asks for your choice through Google's consent form; with your agreement, Google receives technical information about your phone (advertising ID, IP address, model, language, interactions with ads) to show ads, limit how often they repeat, measure them and, if you accept, personalise them. If you refuse, ads are still shown, but non-personalised, or “limited” if you also refuse storage on your phone; Google processes this information under its own rules (policies.google.com/technologies/ads). Your books, your notes, your photos and your quotes are never sent to it. To credit a video, the relay keeps the reference Google sends it. In the European Union, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, you can change your mind at any time: Settings › Ad choices. With “Remove ads”, no ad is shown: only the video is still offered, if you choose it. With BookHunter Plus, no advertising at all.

Purchases

Purchases (“Remove ads” and BookHunter Plus, monthly, yearly or lifetime) go through Google Play, which processes the payment: BookHunter never sees your card or your payment method. To grant you the purchase, the app sends the relay the purchase token issued by Google Play; the relay checks it with Google, then keeps a fingerprint (SHA-256) of that token; for a subscription, it also keeps the token itself, needed to check each renewal. It also keeps the product purchased, its expiry date and your account identifier, without your name or your email address, for as long as the purchase is valid. A refunded purchase stops being granted. To enforce daily limits, the relay counts, per account, searches, assistant readings and photos sent, without their content.

Your account

Your account is created with Google, or with an email address and a password that you confirm. Your email address stays with Google Firebase's sign-in service and is never shown to other people. Your date of birth is used to check that you are 18: we keep only the result, never the date.

What is required

To create an account, you need an email address (or a Google account), a name, a username and to confirm that you are 18: without them, no account. Everything else (photos, notes, recordings, assistant, friends) is optional. No decision that has legal effects for you or significantly affects you is made solely by automated means: the age check and the daily limits apply fixed rules, which you can challenge by writing to us.

Phone permissions

The camera is used to scan an ISBN and to photograph covers, pages and books; the microphone, only when you record yourself reading aloud; notifications, for the reminders you choose, all scheduled on your phone. BookHunter uses neither your location nor your contacts.

Who receives your data

Only the providers that run the service, each for its role described here: Google (Firebase, App Check, AdMob, Google Play), Cloudflare (relay, encrypted copy, photos, assistant), OpenRouter (assistant, as a fallback), the Bibliothèque nationale de France and Open Library (book search, covers). Google, Cloudflare and OpenRouter may process data outside the European Union, notably in the United States: these transfers rely on the European Commission's standard contractual clauses or, for certified companies, on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework; a copy of these safeguards is sent to you on request at ombelle@maxfabrics.com. Open Library (Internet Archive, in the United States) is not our provider: it receives the searches passed on by the relay, without your identity, and your IP address when your phone downloads a cover, as the service you ask for requires. We never sell your data.

Reports and moderation

When you report a person or a piece of content, we receive your report, its reason and the content concerned; the reported person does not know who reported them. Our decisions reach you in the app. Reports and decisions are kept for as long as needed to handle them and to meet our legal obligations.

Sharing and backups

What you choose to send (invitation, quote, backup file or export) goes through the app you choose. A backup contains your library and, if you ask for it, your photos; keep it somewhere safe. The app's data is never copied into Android's backup to Google's cloud; during a direct transfer from one phone to another (cable or Wi-Fi), Android copies it to the new phone, voice recordings included.

How long

Your data stays as long as your account exists. An item moved to the bin stays there for 30 days, then it is erased for good. When you delete your account, its online copy and your photos are erased from the relay immediately, your account, your profile, your username, your friends and your pending requests are erased, and the library on this phone is emptied. A request made by email is handled within 30 days at most.

Correcting or erasing your data

You can edit or delete each item from the app; a deleted book goes to the bin first. To delete your account: Settings › Delete my account. Without the app: maxfabrics.com/ombelle/bookhunter/en/supprimer-mon-compte, or an email to ombelle@maxfabrics.com. You also have the right to access your data, to have it corrected or erased, to restrict its use, to object to it, to withdraw your consent and to get it back in a readable format (portability): the app's backup is one; for the rest, write to ombelle@maxfabrics.com. You can also lodge a complaint with the CNIL (cnil.fr), the French data protection authority, or with the authority of your country.
