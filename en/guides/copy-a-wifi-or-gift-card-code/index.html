Copy a Wi-Fi password or gift card code without retyping it

Twenty characters to copy from a router label or a scratch card, and a typo every other time. There's an easier way.

When there's a QR code

Many recent routers print a Wi-Fi QR code: Android's camera reads it and offers to connect. To share your own Wi-Fi, most recent Android phones show a QR code in the network settings ("Share" button).

When there's only text

A router label without a QR code, a scratch-off gift card, a licence key on a box: text recognition from a photo often copies the whole block, and you still have to pick out the right code. Shoot flat, sharp and without glare, and check the result before pasting.

Classic mix-ups

  • 0 and O, 1, I and l, 5 and S, 8 and B: check these characters one by one.
  • Dashes between groups: some forms want them, others don't.
  • An IBAN has check digits: one wrong digit makes it invalid.

Beware of gift card scams

No legitimate organisation (tax office, bank, tech support, delivery company) asks to be paid in gift card codes. If someone asks you for a code by phone or message, it's a scam: never give it.

Frequently asked questions

How do I copy the Wi-Fi password from my router label?

If there's a QR code, Android's camera reads it. Otherwise, take a photo of the label: Copyglow finds the password in the image and copies it.

How do I avoid confusing 0 and O in a code?

Check those characters one by one. Copyglow flags them and switches to the other reading with one tap.

Can I pay a bill with a gift card?

No. Any organisation asking for payment in gift card codes is a scam.
