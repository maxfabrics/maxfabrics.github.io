How to identify a houseplant and know when to water it

A plant with no label, random watering, yellowing leaves: start from a photo, then water by the plant, not by the calendar.

Take the right photo to identify it

Photo identification works well when the picture is sharp: one whole leaf, facing the camera, in daylight, not backlit. Take a second photo of the whole plant to show its shape (upright, trailing, rosette). A flower, if there is one, helps a lot.

Pl@ntNet, a public science project, recognises tens of thousands of species from photos. Stay critical of the result: compare leaf shape, edges and stem with the suggested photos, and ask someone who knows plants when in doubt.

Water by the plant, not the calendar

"Once a week" is the number one cause of dead houseplants. The right move: push a finger into the soil. If it's dry 2 to 3 cm (about an inch) down for most leafy plants (a bit deeper for cacti and succulents), water. If it's still damp, wait.

The pace changes with:

  • the season: in winter, most plants drink much less;
  • the pot: terracotta dries faster than plastic; without a drainage hole, water sits and roots rot;
  • light: near a bright window, the plant drinks more;
  • the soil: a chunky, well-draining mix dries faster.

Read the signs

  • Yellow, soft leaves and soil that's always wet: too much water. Water less often and check drainage.
  • Dry, crispy leaves and soil pulling away from the pot: not enough water, or very dry air.
  • Long, pale, leaning stems: not enough light.

Think of pets and children

Several very common plants, such as pothos and monstera, are toxic to cats and dogs. Check each plant's toxicity as soon as you've identified it, especially if paws or small hands can reach it.

Frequently asked questions

What's a free app to identify a houseplant?

Pl@ntNet is free and science-based. LeafHunter uses it for photo identification and adds watering reminders tailored to each plant.

How often should I water a houseplant?

There's no single schedule: water when the top few centimetres of soil are dry. Depending on the plant, season and pot, that's anywhere from a few days to several weeks.

Why are my plant's leaves turning yellow?

Most often it's overwatering: soil that never dries, a pot without drainage. Less often, not enough light or fertiliser.

Is pothos toxic to cats?

Yes, pothos is toxic to cats and dogs. Keep it out of reach.
