The envelope method: how to budget step by step

Every dollar or euro gets a job on payday. When an envelope is empty, you know right away it's time to slow down. Here's how to set it up in one evening.

The idea in one sentence

On payday, you split all your income into envelopes (rent, groceries, fun, savings) until nothing is left to assign. Every expense then comes out of its envelope. An empty envelope means: stop, or move money from another one, on purpose.

Step 1: start from what you really spend

Open your last three bank statements and list what comes back every month: rent, power, phone, subscriptions, insurance, transport. Those are your fixed envelopes. Use the real amounts, not the ones you wish you had.

Step 2: everyday envelopes

Groceries, fuel, going out, gifts, surprises. For each, check what you actually spent over the last three months and take the average. Start with 8 to 12 envelopes: more than that and people get lost and give up.

Step 3: sinking funds for big expenses

Holidays, Christmas, car insurance, repairs: these hit once or twice a year and wreck a budget. Divide the yearly amount by twelve and set that share aside every month. A 360 yearly insurance bill becomes a 30 monthly envelope.

Step 4: assign everything down to zero

Fill the fixed envelopes first, then savings, then everyday spending. Money left over goes to savings or sinking funds. If you're short, cut fun first. At the end of the month, whatever is left in an envelope rolls over to the next month: nothing is lost.

Cash or virtual?

Real cash envelopes ("cash stuffing") make every purchase tangible: you watch the envelope get thinner. But they don't work for online payments or rent. Many people do both: cash for groceries and going out, virtual for the rest.

The mistakes that make people quit

  • Ideal amounts instead of real spending: the budget breaks in week one.
  • No "surprises" envelope: the first breakdown becomes a disaster.
  • Too many envelopes: you spend more time sorting than living.
  • Giving up after overspending: moving money between envelopes isn't cheating, it's the method.

Frequently asked questions

How many envelopes should I start with?

Between 8 and 12: fixed bills, everyday spending (groceries, transport, going out), a surprises envelope and savings. You can add more later.

Do I need to connect my bank account?

No. The method works by hand. Envelopio never accesses your bank: you log expenses or import a statement file, read on your phone.

What if an envelope is empty before the end of the month?

Either stop that spending until next payday, or move money from another envelope, choosing which one. Next month, adjust the envelope that ran over.

What happens to money left at the end of the month?

It stays in its envelope and rolls over to next month, or you move it to savings.
